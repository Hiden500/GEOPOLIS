GENERAL FRAMEWORK: 2-A) Countries are divided into 5 categories:
2-AA) Superpowers (only eligible: United States and Soviet Union: these states hold massive industries, massive global influence, massive militaries, they also have effective propaganda generation; as such negotiating with these states is going to be tough with concessions)
2-AB) Secondary powers (these states have developed industries, have high human capitals and have substantial soft-power through media or propaganda, but these states can also be either allied with a superpower or can even be subordinate to a superpower, non-aligned secondary powers are rare, examples for this state include as of scenario starting: United Kingdom, France, Republic of China etc., negotiating with these states would be hard as they would demand strategic benefits)
2-AC) Regional powers (these are the middle states, they boast significant influence in their respective regions but can be subordinated to superpowers, they can struggle against secondary power do rewrite the regional order, these states hold good industries and sizeable militaries capable of operations beyond their homeland but they can face scrutiny from secondary powers or superpowers)
2-AD) General states (these states either are underdeveloped, developing or are developed but are either constrained by their land area, population or other factors, these states are General states which are normal basically and have average stats; they can either be neutral/non-aligned or ally with US or Soviet Blocs., negotiating with these states is very easy)
2-AE) Non-recognized states (these states are unrecognized/disputed, their sovereignty can be taken over and no one will bat an eye, some examples include Princely states of Indian subcontinent, they might or might not have industries or any army at all)
2-AF) The evolution or devolution of a country from these categories will be determined by category stats: 1) International influence out of 100, 2) Total armed forces size out of 100, 3) Economic value out of 100
2-AG) If a country verifies the threshold required for a higher category then it would be promoted to that category

STATS FRAMEWORK: 4-A) Every 6 months ALWAYS prepare a detailed bi-annual report to track previous 6 months performance and overall evaluation
4-B) Economic, military or international decisions can increase or decrease the primary category stats; therefore, a separate YEARLY REPORT shall be generated showcasing the status of all recognized nations across the three primary metrics (International Influence, Total Armed Forces Size, and Economic Value) out of 100, alongside any category promotions or demotions triggered by threshold verification (see 2-AF). This report shall also include a narrative geopolitical summary not exceeding three paragraphs per Superpower and two paragraphs per Secondary Power, noting any dramatic shifts in the global order.
4-C) Stat modifications shall be calculated per bi-annual cycle as follows: positive economic growth (+1 to +5), military modernization or expansion (+1 to +3), successful diplomatic treaties or alliance formations (+1 to +2), territorial acquisition (+2 to +4), and proxy war victory (+1 to +3). Conversely, stat penalties shall apply for: economic recession or sanctions (-1 to -5), military defeat or collapse (-3 to -8), diplomatic isolation (-1 to -3), territorial loss (-2 to -4), and proxy war defeat (-1 to -3).
4-D) The threshold values for category promotion and demotion are as follows: to achieve Superpower status a nation must maintain ≥85 in all three stats; to achieve Secondary Power status a nation must maintain ≥60 in at least two stats and ≥50 in the third; to achieve Regional Power status a nation must maintain ≥40 in at least two stats; General States occupy the 10-39 range; Non-recognized States fall below 10 or lack formal sovereignty. A nation that drops below its current category threshold for two consecutive yearly reports shall be demoted.
4-E) The bi-annual report (see 4-A) shall contain: a) a conflict ledger detailing all wars, battles, and skirmishes with casualty estimates and territorial outcomes; b) a diplomatic ledger summarizing all treaties, alliances broken or formed, and summit results; c) an economic ledger noting GDP trajectory, trade agreement impacts, and aid flows; d) a category shift ledger indicating any promotions or demotions with justification.
